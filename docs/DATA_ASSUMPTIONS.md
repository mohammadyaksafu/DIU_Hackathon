# Synthetic data: assumptions (guideline §11)

Generated by `backend/pipelines/generate_data.py`, seed 42: 2,000 customers (19 of them recruited into structuring) + 103 mule / one-off scam wallets, 285 merchants, 153 agents, 31 enterprises (30 employers + 1 shell), 90 days from 2026-06-01 (Asia/Dhaka), ≈183k transactions, ≈1.2% labelled fraud.

## Normal behaviour
| Persona | Share | Typical behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Student | 20% | Cash-in from agent on the 1st and 15th; recharges, small payments, transfers to friends |
| Garments worker | 22% | Salary disbursement around day 7; cash-out of 40–80% within 30 h; remittance to family |
| Professional | 22% | Salary around day 1; bills; larger merchant payments; family remittance |
| Remittance receiver | 12% | Receives monthly family transfers, cashes out within 24 h, mostly USSD (vulnerable segment) |
| Shopkeeper | 12% | Receives payments from local customers; evening cash-outs of 5k–24.9k (legit near-limit) |
| Online (f-commerce) seller | 5% | Many first-time senders per day, daily cash-out: **legit look-alike of a mule** |
| Freelancer | 12% | Irregular income twice a month; more night activity (25%) |

- Diurnal hour weights peak 10:00–13:00 and 18:00–22:00. Amounts are log-normal per customer scale; 35% of P2P amounts are rounded to 500.
- 10% of customers (50% of sellers) open accounts **during** the window (legit young accounts).
- Contacts: 2–5 per customer (70% same division); 3–8 favourite merchants; 1–2 home agents.
- Legit noise: 1.5% change phones (40% of them with a legit SIM replacement + PIN reset, 50% followed by a large purchase); 3% reset passwords.

## Injected fraud (label = 1)
| Scenario | Mechanics |
|---|---|
| S1 Account takeover | SIM swap 1–10 h before, PIN reset 5–40 min before, new device, other division; 2–4 drains of 4–12× usual amount, 70% at night |
| S2 Prize scam | Round amount (500–5,000) to a ring collector (35% to a one-off rented wallet); 30% pay a second "fee" |
| S3 Refund scam | Ring member sends 200–1,200 bait; victim "refunds" 2,000–9,000 to another ring wallet within 3–30 min (bait labelled too) |
| S4 Mule ring | 10 rings of 5–10 wallets (50% are old rented accounts). Collectors forward 90–98% within 5–50 min; forwarders cash out at ring agents within 10–120 min |
| S5 Structuring | 3–5 colluding wallets receive a shell-company disbursement, then 2–4 cash-outs of 23,600–24,990 at one agent |
| S6 Social engineering (OTP) | Same device, 1.5–6× usual amount, rounded to 500, to a ring collector |

Victims are drawn with higher weight from remittance receivers (×3), garments workers (×2) and students (×1.2). The last ring is active in the final 11 days so the live demo has an active mule ring.

## Evaluation hygiene
- Time split: train days 0–69, validation 70–79 (early stopping, calibration, thresholds), **test 80–89** (touched once).
- Features are point-in-time: computed before the transaction is added to state; graph snapshots use only edges before the snapshot time.
- Segment attributes (division, age, KYC, persona) are **not** model inputs; they are used only for the fairness audit.
- Raw clock hour is excluded from the model (it acted as a dataset artefact); behavioural `s_hour_freq` / `is_night` are used instead.

## Known limitations
Patterns are designed by us, so the model can learn them more easily than real fraud. Real fraud adapts, labels are delayed and noisy, and base rates are far lower. These results demonstrate the pipeline, not production performance.
